A prisoner's dilemma lab

Stool
pigeon.

Two pigeons get busted with a stolen baguette. Separate cages, same offer: rat the other one out and walk away fat. Both keep quiet, both get a decent share. Both rat, both get scraps.

Easy, once. Now do it two hundred times with the same bird.

Snitches get crumbs.

The deal, in crumbs

Whatever they do, ratting pays more.

If they keep quiet, ratting gets you 5 instead of 3. If they rat, ratting gets you 1 instead of 0. So both rat, and both walk off with 1 when they could have had 3. That is the dilemma.

Play it once and ratting wins. Play it again and again with the same bird and memory starts to matter: what they did last time, whether you can trust them, whether they hold a grudge.

1980

Robert Axelrod asked game theorists to mail him programs that play this game, then made them fight. Fourteen entries, some of them hundreds of lines long. The winner had four: cooperate first, then copy whatever the other one did. He ran it again with 62 entries. Same winner.

            Language
            
        
              
        Scripts run once per round and must return C (keep quiet) or return D (rat). If nothing returns, you keep quiet. No loops, so nothing can hang.
        
              
        
                
        round
        This round's number, starting at 1. You never know how many are left.
        
                
        their.last my.last
        Last move, or none in round 1.
        
                
        their[2]
        Two moves ago. their[1] is the last one.
        
                
        their.at(3)
        Their move in round 3.
        
                
        their.first
        Their opening move.
        
                
        their.cooperations their.defections
        Totals so far.
        
                
        their.count(D, 5)
        How many D in their last 5 moves. Leave out the 5 for all of them.
        
                
        their.rate(C)
        Share of C so far, 0 to 1. Takes an optional window too.
        
                
        their.ever(D)
        Have they ever played D?
        
                
        their.streak
        How many times in a row they repeated their last move.
        
                
        my.score my.payoff
        Your total, and what you got last round.
        
                
        T R P S

        chance(0.1)
